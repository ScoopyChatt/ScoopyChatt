## Post 2 — pairs with the pet waste stations article

**Suggested groups:** property manager / landlord groups, HOA board groups, Chattanooga business groups

> For anyone on an HOA board or managing a pet-friendly property: the reason your
> waste stations are not working is almost never the number of stations.
>
> Two things actually decide it. First, distance. A station more than about 150 feet
> off the route people already walk their dogs gets used a fraction as much as one
> directly on it. Walk your property and look for the worn paths in the grass. That is
> where the stations belong, not where the site plan put them.
>
> Second, restocking. An empty bag dispenser or an overflowing can teaches residents
> the rule is not enforced, and compliance falls off fast after that. Whoever services
> them has to get there before they run out, not after somebody complains.
>
> Rough starting point on count: one station per 40 to 50 pet-friendly units.
>
> We do walkthroughs for HOAs and apartment communities around Chattanooga and North
> Georgia at no charge, whether or not you hire anybody.
>
> More detail: scoopychatt.com/blog/pet-waste-stations-apartments-hoas-chattanooga

## Post 3 — pairs with the party cleanup article

**Suggested groups:** Chattanooga and North Georgia community groups, event/party planning groups

> Party season PSA for dog owners: do not save the yard cleanup for the morning of.
>
> The day before is the right call. Close enough that the dog cannot undo it, far
> enough that the grass dries out and the smell has time to fade. Because that is the
> part people forget in August. Clearing the waste kills the source, but a heavily used
> yard still holds odor in the grass and topsoil for a bit. The fix is not a spray, it
> is time plus a good watering after.
>
> Order of operations that works: mow two or three days out, clean up the day before,
> water the heavy spots, done.
>
> If you would rather not do it yourself, one-time cleanups start at $85, no contract
> and nothing to cancel after. We run seven days a week across Chattanooga and North
> Georgia, so a Friday or Saturday before a weekend party is usually doable. Ask by
> Tuesday or Wednesday to be safe, but ask anyway if it is later than that.

## Post 4 — pairs with the yard odor article

**Suggested groups:** Chattanooga and North Georgia community groups, local dog owner groups, lawn and garden groups

> If your yard still smells after you scoop it, you did not miss anything. The smell
> is coming out of the ground, not off the waste.
>
> Bacteria and ammonia compounds soak into the thatch and the top inch of soil and
> stay there after the solids are gone. Our humidity keeps them lifting off the
> ground instead of staying put, and the clay soil around here drains slow enough
> that it all sits near the surface. A dry yard in a dry climate stops smelling on
> its own. Ours does not.
>
> The fix most people skip: after you clear it, soak the bad areas. Really soak
> them, like a heavy rain, not a sprinkle. That carries the compounds down past the
> root zone instead of leaving them where your nose is. Give it two or three days.
> For a yard that gets cleaned on any kind of regular schedule, that is the whole
> thing, and it costs nothing.
>
> What does not work: lime (not an odor treatment, and it will burn your lawn if you
> overdo it), scented sprays (a few hours, then you have two smells), bleach (please
> do not).
>
> We do sell a deodorizing treatment for the yards where water alone will not cut it,
> but if yours is on a normal schedule you honestly do not need it. Try the hose first.
>
> Full rundown: scoopychatt.com/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-dog-poop-smell-in-yard-chattanooga
